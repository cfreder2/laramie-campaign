Company Logbook

Session 1 — The Town Meeting (in-world Day 1), from the Company's own notes
Laramie City, W.T. · Played 27 June 2026 · What was done, and what came of it
Dawn — At the Station John

Reading the paper at the depot, John watched the schoolteacher, Miss Hartley, cross the platform — a striking woman who caught his eye, very well put together and professional, a line of children following at her heels, clearly in command. Close by stood one of the biggest men he'd ever seen, "Big Steve," talking low with Marshal Moyer about getting "the deed" back. John offered the big man his help — "I'm good at adjusting things" — and was waved off, with a word to steer clear of Two-Dollar Jack. He trailed the Marshal and stepped into Ivinson's store.

Dawn — A Hard Errand Nickles

Nickles, a known face about town, was set by his father, Two-Dollar Jack, to ready his horse — the Clap — and cart off the late Mrs. Boothe. The Madam grudgingly spared him some food. As he was loading the woman's body onto the cart, he saw and heard Big Steve approaching. When Steve came into view, Nickles hastily pulled the cart away; they traded a few words as they parted, and he hurried to put as much distance as he could between himself and Big Steve — remembering the time Steve had clapped his ears.

Dawn — A Soured Claim Stinky Pete

Pete woke to find his tools moved and a broken beaded necklace left at camp — Arapaho work. His morning dig turned up nothing but a dead rabbit at the bottom of the mine shaft, which he took as a warning of what would happen to him if he kept digging. He went hunting for the Arapaho camp, could not find it, and headed to town; dwarves on their way out told him the diggings were cursed. (Pete — son of Ragnar, son of Kagnar, son of Durin who mined in Moria — works what he calls his Blessed Mine.)

Morning — A Job at Ivinson's John

Inside the store John asked after work, and Ivinson had some to offer: help move the schoolhouse, which stood square in the path of the coming roundhouse. John took the job and set off for the school to see it done.

Midday — Where the Roads Cross The Company

On his way to the school, John ran into Stinky Pete, and the two dwarves took to each other at once — though at arm's length, for Pete more than lived up to his name. They carried on together, the school lying on the far side of the burial grounds. There the pair bumped into Nickles for the first time, his rickety wagon in poor repair; Pete, a craftsman, patched it back to sound condition. Then the three parted for their own errands — John on to the schoolhouse, Nickles swinging in a wide arc around Big Steve Long toward the Wide Grave, and Pete climbing to the top of the burial mounds.

Midday — On the Burial Mounds Stinky Pete

Pete had an audience. Four figures stood on that ground — Big Steve Long, come out from Clara Boothe's house nearby, and the three companions — and all of them paused to see what the strange dwarf would do. Then, instead of cursing the ground and desecrating it, Pete had a change of heart. He laid the dead rabbit there as a warning of his own, and a shaft of light fell upon it. Heading back, he passed railway men carrying a dead body toward town.

Midday — Fine Lessons John

Hearing children at their happy spelling, John fell in beside the schoolteacher as they ran out to play. "Those are fine lessons," he told her, and offered to help move the schoolhouse — she said plainly it would NOT be moved. He agreed instead to work for her and bring lumber to finish it where it stood. He was to go back to Ivinson and buy the lumber on her account, and she gave him a paper with the order written out to hand to the storekeeper.

Afternoon — Alone at the Wide Grave Nickles

At the Wide Grave, Nickles set to his work and marked the wolves waiting patiently at the treeline. He stripped the body of its fine clothes and its bedsheet, and saw that the woman had been strangled. As he dug, a few of the wolves grew bolder and stepped out of the clearing, and he worked the faster for it. He threw a single shovelful of dirt over Clara in the shallow grave, then rode off — and the wolves swarmed her body and began to feed. He kept what he had taken: the fine clothes, and the bedsheet too.

Late Afternoon — The Boy and the Necklace Stinky Pete

Back at camp Pete found a young Arapaho boy sitting on a stump by his campfire, warming himself. Though they shared no language, the two understood one another well enough; Pete returned the necklace, and the boy shared the rabbit he had been cooking in the fire. As the boy walked off, another shaft of light shone upon him, and he disappeared into the forest. Pete then worked the claim a long five hours — the dice unkind but for one good strike — and pulled about nine ounces of silver, near twelve dollars' worth.

Late Afternoon — A "Fair" Price John

John brought Miss Hartley's order to Ivinson. The storekeeper was disappointed to hear the schoolhouse would not be moved, but happy enough to sell the lumber at what he called a fair price — two dollars a board. John tried to haggle, growing more frustrated and more sure he was being swindled; he left and came back twice before he finally agreed to two dollars a stick, with several trainloads needed to fill the whole order. Only then did he learn the wood was not in the back stock at all, but arriving the next morning by train.

Late Afternoon — The Back Door Nickles

Just as John was finishing at Ivinson's and stepping out of the store, he saw Nickles come up the street with the wagon. Nickles drove around to the back, gave a secret knock at the rear entrance, and was let in by an armed goblin. Inside he dealt with Mrs. Ivinson — a gruff, unpleasant business, her husband being the easier man by far — but he came away with four dollars for the fine clothes and a standing promise of information (he passed along the tale of Pete and his "Indian trouble") — "information," as she put it plainly, always has value to Mrs. Ivinson.

Late Afternoon — Larceny John

While Nickles was inside haggling over Clara's clothes, John helped himself to the cart and Clap. He drove down to the river and followed the bank to the rail bridge, where he crossed paths with Pete again, headed into town. John talked him into it, and the two turned back for Pete's camp with the horse and wagon.

Late Afternoon — A Cold Trail Nickles

Nickles came out to find the wagon gone. He tried to track it through the mud, but the trail died where it met the road, and for all his searching he could not tell which way it had gone. Flustered, and dreading what his father would do, he wandered back toward the Bucket — having let Clap be stolen out from under him.

Late Afternoon — Smoke Stinky Pete & the Company

At Pete's camp, he and John fell to planning how to "retrieve" the lumber off the train. They reckoned it best to drive the cart wide around the town and make camp far enough out that, if it came to an explosion, no one would hear it. As they talked, the Arapaho boy stepped out of the shadows behind Pete — as though he had formed from the smoke of the fire — and joined them. Though none of them shared a language, John made the plan plain enough: they meant to blow up a train. At that the boy drew a knife and cut his own arm, with the fierce resolve of a warrior, then signed that he would take the cart and make the camp. John chose to trust him, and the boy took the cart and Clap and headed east — to set the ambush, they supposed.

Late Afternoon — Cleaning Up Nickles

Frantic over the lost horse, Nickles slipped back to the dead woman's house, took some vegetables, and carried them to the Bucket of Blood bundled in Clara's bedsheet — then set to tidying, fretting his father would beat him for the missing Clap. The vegetables went into the stew pot that night, and every soul who ate of it was taken with the runs.

Late Afternoon — Outfitting Stinky Pete & John

Right before the store closed for the night, Pete used the silver from his mine to buy a Colt Navy pistol and 50 rounds; John bought a lasso.

Dusk — An Arrangement The Company

Right before the meeting, John and Pete came by and squared a deal with Nickles: help them with a job the next morning, out east on the tracks, and they would pay him by finding his horse and cart in return. And while he was working for someone, Two-Dollar Jack would hold off any beating — so the arrangement bought Nickles some time over the missing Clap besides. With that settled, they made for Keystone Hall as the sun went down.

Night — The Town Meeting The Company

Near the whole town turned out — Big Steve; Marshal Moyer, well armed; Ivinson beside a well-dressed railroad man, the Superintendent; Father Dominic in his Paylor robes with his apprentices; and Amos Holt, back from keeping the natives off. Moyer asked the Paylor to say a prayer for Mrs. Boothe, and Father Dominic praised her generosity of spirit — for giving nearly all her worldly possessions to the Church of Paylor, and for her devout service to its will. In honor of her passing, Moyer declared the roundhouse would be named the Boothe Roundhouse, to remember her generosity for all time.

Talk turned to order in the town. The Moyers would be cleaning up the streets, and they expected obedience and lawfulness in the days to come — for the commissioner of the Western railway would not set his roundhouse in a city run by thieves. Moyer alluded to some cleanup needed at the Bucket of Blood.

Next came the matter of the three deeds: the first Miss Hartley's, the second the late Clara Boothe's, and the third a soldier's from Fort Sanders who had made his home across the creek north of the fort — and who was not in attendance. As Moyer droned on about Clara having no next of kin and her deed passing to the city, Miss Hartley spoke up. She declared she would not move the school, then shocked the room by producing Clara's deed — given to her for safekeeping, she said, should anything happen to Clara, as the two of them both cared for the children. She offered that the roundhouse could be built on the north side of town, a prospect the "respectable" sort did not care for. Sharp-eyed, Nickles marked James Two-Rivers — a half-elf who speaks Arapaho — watching Eve intently as she spoke. After some back and forth, the meeting broke up, with more "follow-up" promised on the fate of the deeds.

Night — The Back-Alley Brawl The Company

As the meeting broke and Eve left, the company saw Big Steve gesture two outsiders to follow after her. John slipped ahead and set a trap in the alley to wait for them. They caught up; John told Eve to run, and the fight was on. The first thug, Bull, stumbled into John's trap and turned his ankle.

The second was a woman — Pearl, a hired gun. Pete came at her from behind, but his first swing missed badly — and she spun and fired a single shot clean through his belly, ten hard points of it. Only his grit kept him standing; bleeding and near death, he steadied himself and stayed in the fight. With his newly bought Colt he cowed Pearl into surrender while Nickles grappled her fast. Bull fared worse: after a beating from the flat of John's blade, a single strike of Pete's pick finished him.

Off Pearl, Nickles took a folded paper, a bowie knife, and eleven dollars. He could not read the paper himself, but the company made it out later — "Half payment up front now; the other half when you return with the papers" — and Nickles knew the stationery for the Bucket of Blood's. Bull's body yielded five dollars, a bowie knife, and a heap of chewing tobacco. The slain man was buried beyond town, deep in the ground thanks to Pete's digging. Pearl — a hired stranger, brought to town blindfolded and knowing no one — was kept in the company's charge; whether they can convince her to help them remains to be seen. Eve was unharmed — thankful, and wanting nothing more to do with the violence, she went home for the night.

Night — Read In The Company

Once the fight in the alley was settled, Pete told Nickles the truth — that John had been the one to make off with his cart and Clap. Nickles struck John to the ground and grappled him; John gave it up at once, told the truth, and talked Nickles around — it would all be worth it, he swore. With that the company headed east out of the city to make camp, hoping to rendezvous again with the Arapaho boy — and with Clap and the cart — their plan to take the lumber before it reached town still forming in their minds. Pearl, their captive, they brought along with them. There the first day ended.

And with it, the whole company rose to 2nd level.
