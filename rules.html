Money & Coin

A gold piece is a dollar. Nothing on your sheet changes.

The D&D coin pyramid maps onto 1868 U.S. currency exactly:

Slang: "two bits" = 25¢, "an eagle" = $10, "a double eagle" = $20. A laborer earns about $2 a day — which is where Two-Dollar Jack got his name.

Bullion & the Assay (gold by weight)

Raw gold dust and nuggets aren't coin — they're weighed at the bank, and the scale's honesty is a live question.

Assay & exchange fee: ~5% to turn dust into coin. Greenback paper trades at only ~72¢ on the gold dollar — the frontier distrusts paper. A crooked scale can shave weight: Wisdom (Insight) or Intelligence (Investigation) DC 13 to notice.

Drink & the Cups Track

There is no free cup in Laramie. Every drink carries a boon and a bane, and both apply the moment it goes down.

The Cups Track

Only beer and spirits advance the track — coffee, smoke, and sarsaparilla do not make a man drunk. Each alcoholic drink is one step (rotgut counts as two). The boons stand; the banes of drunkenness pile up.

DrinkStateEffect
1WarmedBoons apply cleanly. Only the drink's own bane.
2Loosened−1 to attack rolls and ability checks; advantage on saves vs. being Frightened (the courage rising).
3TipsyDisadvantage on Dexterity checks and Dexterity saving throws.
4DrunkDisadvantage on attack rolls; −2 AC; but immune to the Frightened condition.
5+UnderDC 15 Con save per further drink or gain a level of exhaustion. A second failure: out cold.

Recovery: one step down per hour without drinking; a full night's rest clears it (and one level of exhaustion).

Does a second drink stack?

  • Same effect never stacks (standard 5e): temp HP takes the higher roll, never added; advantage is just advantage; disadvantage is just disadvantage. Two beers = the better 1d4 and a refreshed hour, not 2d4.
  • Different effects all apply at once. A whiskey and a coffee = both boons and both banes together.
  • The track is the real cost. Drunkenness accumulates; the boons do not.

The Wound System

Hit points are endurance and luck. Wounds are the damage a body cannot ignore. This is why getting shot is serious.

A Wound is inflicted when:

  • A firearm hit deals 10+ damage → Con save, DC 10 + half the damage, or take a Wound.
  • A Brutal melee hit (tomahawk, war lance, felling axe, the auto-pick) deals 10+ → same save. A hatchet wounds like a bullet.
  • Any critical hit with any weapon → a Wound, no save.
WoundsEffect (they stack)
1Disadvantage on all attack rolls
2Speed halved; disadvantage on Dex saves
3Incapacitated — no actions/bonus/reactions; half move; whisper only
4Dying — drop to 0 HP, begin death saves

Recovery: short rest + DC 12 Medicine (healer's kit) removes 1 Wound; a long rest removes all.

Guns, Range & Rate of Fire

TermAt the table
Misfire 1Natural 1 to hit = the gun jams. A full Action clears it. Watch for an enemy's 1 — that's your window.
RepeatingFires every round, no reload between shots. Revolvers (6) and the Winchester (15) — you won't empty either in a fight.
Single-ShotMusket, Sharps, derringer, each shotgun barrel: your next Action is reloading. You fire every other round.
Shotgun ConeNot aimed — a 15-ft cone, Dex save (no armor bonus). Within 5 ft, disadvantage on the save. Useless past 15 ft. Double-barrel fires both for double dice.

Range Bands = Accuracy

Within a weapon's first range number = normal. Between the two = disadvantage. Past the second = can't hit. A rifle's bands are huge, so a rifleman fires clean where a pistolero is at disadvantage or out of range — that is rifle accuracy.

  • Handy — sidearms ignore the disadvantage for firing within 5 ft of an enemy (hip-shooting); long guns don't. Pistols own the clinch.
  • Braced — a rifle/bow with the Steady the Bead aim knack ignores long-range disadvantage. Rifles own the distance.

Cover

When guns are drawn, the first question is rarely "what do I shoot" — it's "what am I behind." Cover is the difference between a fight you walk away from and one you don't.

CoverExamplesBenefit
Halfhitching rail, trough, a body between you+2 AC, +2 Dex saves
Three-Quartersdoorframe, barrel, porch post, a rise+5 AC, +5 Dex saves
Totala wall, a boulder, around a cornercan't be hit — can't shoot out

Firearms shed 3 AC off heavy armor and 1 off medium; shields give only +1 against guns. Dynamite and both barrels of a shotgun can remove cover. On the battle-mats, the crates, troughs, and overturned tables earn their keep.

Frontier Knacks — Learned Abilities

Knacks are small techniques anyone can be taught: 3 days of downtime with a teacher and a DC 13 check in the governing ability.

Steady the Bead (Aim Down Sights)

Bonus Action. With a ranged weapon, gain advantage on your next attack this turn against a target in normal range. Cost: you can't have moved >5 ft and can't move after; if you take damage before the shot, you lose it; ranged weapons only. (A Wound's disadvantage cancels the advantage to a straight roll — aim undoes the shake but can't overcome it.)

Other knacks to learn: Fan the Hammer, Fast Draw, Roll With It, Read the Room — each a real benefit with a real cost and a teacher who has a reason to share it.

Grit & Luck

You already have this. Every character carries a pool of Grit — raw frontier luck and stubbornness. You don't earn it; it's yours from the start.

How much you have

Your Grit pool equals your proficiency bonus:

Level (≈ Day)Grit you carry
1–42 Grit
5–83 Grit
9+4 Grit

Right now, on Day 1, you have 2 Grit in your pocket. Mark it on your sheet; spend it down as a tally.

What you spend it on

Spending 1 Grit takes no action — you can even do it on someone else's turn. Choose one:

  • Steady the nerve — add 1d6 to any d20 roll (attack, save, or check) after you see the number, before the DM says hit or miss.
  • Quick hands — reload a firearm as a bonus action instead of an action.
  • Ride your luck — turn a critical hit against you into an ordinary hit (and cancel the Wound it would have caused). The shot that should have killed you only grazed.

Getting it back

Regain all spent Grit on a long rest (a proper night's sleep). Optional table rule: the DM may hand back a single Grit for a moment of real daring or great play — luck favors the bold.

Hard Living — the frontier's tax (this one isn't yours to spend)

The opposite of Grit. Not a resource — a hazard. After a genuinely hard night — rotgut and no real sleep, a night exposed without shelter, a brutal beating — make a DC 12 Constitution save or gain one level of exhaustion. Food, warmth, and rest pay it back down. The frontier collects what you owe it.
